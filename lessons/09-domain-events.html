Domain events: the domain tells the world

The business rules do not shout into a void — they publish what happened, and anyone who cares may listen.

1 · Hook — a question before any jargon

You marry two people. The news of the wedding does not happen inside the couple alone — it spreads: invitations were sent, the newspaper prints the announcement, relatives update their plans. The marriage itself did not need to know every guest's reaction; it simply happened, and the world reacted.

Domain events are exactly that: "something important happened" — a past-tense fact the domain publishes, without knowing who listens. In Astralia the domain already computes facts like dst-ambiguous warnings; this lesson designs the events that would tell the rest of the system.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to design a domain event on paper — Given/When/Then, past tense, immutable, published without knowing its subscribers.

3 · The concept in small steps

New words this lesson: 3

  • Domain event — a past-tense fact about what happened in the domain.
  • Publisher / Subscriber — the event's source and its (unknown) listeners.
  • Event name — a past-participle phrase, e.g., BirthDataSaved.

TS basics — a record is a box of data

A TypeScript object is a box of named values. A domain event is a frozen box: a name (past tense) plus the facts, with no methods that mutate it. If you have used as const or a readonly type, you already know the shape.

Step 1 — A fact, not a command

In plain words: a domain event says "this already happened" — it never tells anyone what to do.

Precise definition: a domain event is an immutable, past-tense record of something that happened in the domain, published by its source without knowing who, if anyone, subscribes to it (after Fowler's classic definition).

The analogy — the newspaper headline: the newspaper prints "Bridge Reopened After Repairs" — it does not print "Commuters, please now cross". The fact is published; readers decide what to do. An event is a headline, not an instruction.

Where this analogy stops being true

A newspaper knows its readers exist and can be asked to subscribe; a domain event is published to zero or many listeners, and the publisher never learns their names. Also, events in code must carry the facts (ids, timestamps), not ink.

What is a domain event?

Step 2 — The event name is past tense

In plain words: name events like headlines: BirthDataSaved, DstAmbiguousWarningRaised — done, not requested.

Precise definition: an event name is a past-participle phrase describing the fact the domain publishes; the event carries the data needed by subscribers (aggregate id, values, timestamp), never internal implementation details.

The analogy — the wedding invitation (from the hook): the invitation says "the marriage took place" (fact) and lists who, where, when — not "please arrange the chairs". The invitation is printed once and sent to many; the couple does not track every guest.

Where this analogy stops being true

Invitations are sent to a known guest list; events are published to an unknown audience. Also, an invitation is paper — events are immutable records that subscribers may read long after publishing.

Which shape fits an event?

Step 3 — Publish without knowing subscribers

In plain words: the source records the fact and walks away. If nobody listens, the world still moved; if many listen, the source does not care.

Precise definition: the publisher emits the event and holds no reference to subscribers; subscribers receive a copy and react (send an email, update a projection, trigger a process). Decoupling is the point: changing who listens never changes the publisher.

The analogy — Fowler's credit-card story: a bank processes a charge; the fact "the charge happened" is recorded. Other systems react — fraud check, accounting, receipts — but the charge processing does not call each one by name.

Where this analogy stops being true

Credit-card systems often need strong guarantees (the fraud check must run); a naive event pub/sub gives none. Events buy decoupling, not delivery guarantees — the guarantees are a separate design decision.

4 · Worked example — I do

I will design two domain events for Astralia on paper, out loud, with Given/When/Then. Follow the reasoning; you will design one event yourself.

"Event 1: BirthDataSaved. Given a valid birth data that passes the invariant guard; when SaveBirthData persists through IBirthDataRepository; then the domain publishes BirthDataSaved with the id, the normalized values, and a timestamp. Past tense, frozen fields, no methods. It does not say 'send a welcome email' — it says 'a birth record was saved'."

"Event 2: DstAmbiguousWarningRaised. Given birth data in a local time that the UT converter marks 'ambiguous'; when SaveBirthData applies the DST policy; then the domain publishes DstAmbiguousWarningRaised with the warning code, the date, and the timestamp. WarningCode is a primitive list, not an object — the warning is a signal, not an aggregate."

What I noticed: both events say "already happened" and carry the facts a subscriber would need. Neither names a subscriber. The domain stays quiet about who reacts — that is the decoupling.

5 · Guided practice — we do

Three design questions with less help each time. Answer before opening the suggestion.

Q1 — worked with me. Which facts belong on BirthDataSaved?

My reasoning: a subscriber (say, a projection or a notifier) needs the identity and the essentials: the aggregate id, the normalized values, the timestamp. It does not need the Drizzle row or the SQL. Facts, not internals.

Answer 1 — check after you try

id, normalized values, and timestamp — the facts subscribers need, with no repository or SQL internals.

Q2 — half-worked. Where does the warning become a signal?

Starter of the reasoning: SaveBirthData receives a WarningCode[] from BirthData.create(). Complete the thought: the warning is a ______ (a signal), not an entity — it has no identity or lifecycle. When the policy sees 'dst-ambiguous', it publishes ______.

Answer 2 — check after you try

The warning is a primitive signal (WarningCode[]), not an entity — no identity, no lifecycle. When the policy sees 'dst-ambiguous', it publishes DstAmbiguousWarningRaised as an immutable event.

Q3 — your turn to finish. Why must the source never know its subscribers?

Start: if SaveBirthData named its subscribers (the email service, the projection), a new listener would force a change to SaveBirthData. Complete the last step: the publisher publishes the fact and ______; adding a subscriber becomes a change to ______, not to ______.

Answer 3 — check after you try

The publisher publishes the fact and walks away; adding a subscriber becomes a change to the subscriber side, not to the publisher. The source's lack of knowledge is the decoupling.

7 · Feedback + wrap-up

You should now be able to say:

  • "A domain event is a past-tense, immutable fact — a headline, not a command."
  • "The publisher never knows its subscribers; adding a listener changes the subscriber side only."
  • "WarningCode[] is a primitive signal, not an entity; the DST warning can become a domain event."

Where the newspaper analogy breaks (recap): newspapers know their readers; events do not know their subscribers. And events buy decoupling, not delivery guarantees.
