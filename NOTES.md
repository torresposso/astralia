# Student notes

Preferences and learning context. When in doubt, these notes win.

## Profile (updated 2026-07-31)

- **Level**: junior in TypeScript, in programming generally, and in DDD —
  self-described. Assume zero prior jargon; define every term on first use and
  recycle it across lessons.
- **Preferred explanation style**: "explain like I'm 5" (ELI5) foundational
  explanations — simple, warm, never condescending. Under-informed, not
  under-intelligent: give a plain-language definition first, then the precise
  definition, then an analogy, and always say where the analogy stops being
  true.

## Preferences

- **Conversation**: Spanish (Colombian costeño), relaxed and direct.
- **Lesson materials**: ENGLISH (per request on 2026-07-31). DDD terms stay in
  English (bounded context, value object, aggregate…). Conversation with the
  assistant stays in Spanish.
- **Course size**: 10 lessons (01-10), organized in five 30-minute sessions.
  Each lesson follows the same 30-minute skeleton: hook, 60-second retrieval,
  concept in small steps, worked example (I do), guided practice (we do),
  independent practice (you do), feedback and wrap-up.
- **Strongest language**: TypeScript. Examples must be read in TS and map to
  Astralia's real syntax.
- **DDD level**: absolute beginner. Every term is defined the first time it
  appears, with a repo example.
- **Learning style**: best with your own code as a case study rather than
  generic book examples. Each lesson must touch real files in `src/`.
- **Relationship with AI**: wants to direct, question, and **veto** architectural
  decisions made by the AI, not accept them blindly. Every conclusion must be
  backed by evidence (file, symbol, test) and by the criteria in `MISSION.md`.

## Practice style

- Cold recall (blank page, 60 seconds) before opening the material.
- Answer before looking at the file; explain the reason before naming the path.
- Record uncertainty in `learning-records/` instead of peeking immediately.
- Interleave model, identity, boundary, and AI-veto questions in every review.
